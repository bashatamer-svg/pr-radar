How it works

Your daily brand & reputation board

PR Radar watches the news for stories about Vodafone Egypt and its main competitors (Orange, WE, e&) — plus mobile-market news that names no brand, and every Vodafone mention including regulatory ones. It reads each story and lays out what matters today — and what to do about it. New stories are checked for every 30 minutes. Here's how to read it.

📈 The pulse, at the top

A one-glance health check for the day.

Big number
How many negative Vodafone stories today. 0 with a green CLEAR badge means a calm day; a red ALERT means something needs eyes.
Competitor tiles
Orange, WE and e& — how much they're in the news and how many of those stories were wins for them (their positive coverage). This is market awareness, not your to-do list.

🗂️ The three lanes

Every story is sorted by what you should do about it.

Needs a response
Negative Vodafone stories — the ones worth addressing.
Wins to amplify
Positive Vodafone stories — good news worth boosting.
Market & noted
Everything about competitors, plus neutral items and sector-wide mobile-market news (price moves, nationwide outages — even when no brand is named) — useful context, no action needed.

🃏 Reading a story card

Each card tells you what it is, how big it is, and what to do.

Positive Negative
The tone of the story (also shown as the coloured badge), read for the brand the story is about — the same scale for everyone. A rival's award is Positive (positive for them); a rival's outage is Negative. What a competitor story means for us is carried by Reach and by its lane, not by the badge — competitor stories always sit in Market & noted.
Reach ●●●○○
How big / impactful the story is, 1 to 5. More dots = more people likely see it.
92% confidence
How sure the AI was about its read. Below 50% turns amber · low — worth a quick double-check.
◱ 2 outlets
How many outlets ran it. The more outlets, the bigger the story.
What to do
with this
Read (what it means) · Audience (who cares) · Action (the recommended move).
2h ago · By …
When it broke and who wrote it. When an article carries no individual byline, the card shows "<Outlet> · newsroom" — the story is desk-written. Coverage expands the full list of outlets.

📡 How stories get in

Two scans on different clocks, then a fixed set of rules every story passes through.

Every 30 min
A fast sweep for anything urgent. It checks the 10 brand & market searches only — the ones that would catch a crisis — so it stays quick and cheap. Anything scored Reach 5 is emailed (and sent by WhatsApp where configured) within the half hour. No daily brief is sent on these runs.
Once a day
08:00 Cairo (07:00 in winter). The full sweep — all 30 sources: the 10 brand & market searches, 7 outlet-specific searches, and 13 newspaper RSS feeds. This run also sends the daily brief and fills in missing bylines.
So, how fresh?
A big story is usually on the board within 30 minutes. A quieter one from a specialist outlet may wait for the morning run. Nothing is only checked once — the 30-minute poll runs all day.

Every story then passes these checks, in order:

1 · Recent
Published in the last 48 hours. Older items are ignored, so a feed republishing its archive can't flood the board.
2 · A real article
Tag, keyword, search and category pages are thrown out — they look like stories in a feed but have no article behind them.
3 · On topic
The 13 newspaper feeds carry everything a paper publishes — football, crime, weather. Those are filtered to items naming an operator or a mobile-market subject before anything else happens. The brand searches skip this: the search already scoped them.
4 · Not a repeat
The same story from several outlets becomes one card. It's matched by headline, then again by the AI's own one-line summary, and finally by an AI check for "same event, different words" — which catches an Arabic and an English write-up of one story. Duplicates aren't thrown away: each extra outlet is added to that card's Coverage list, which is where "◱ N outlets" comes from.
5 · Read & scored
The AI reads what's left and sets the brand, tone, Reach 1–5 and the What to do with this brief.
6 · Worth your time
Anything the AI judges not relevant — wrong country, a look-alike company, a policy story naming no operator — is dropped, and so is anything at Reach 1. The board starts at Reach 2.
7 · Byline
The article is opened to find who wrote it. When there genuinely is no byline the card reads "<Outlet> · newsroom" rather than guessing a name.
